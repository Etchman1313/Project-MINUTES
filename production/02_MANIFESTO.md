# PROJECT MINUTES
## MANIFESTO

**Status:** Active

**Project:** The Uncivil War – Interactive Experience

---

# Why We Exist

Project MINUTES exists to create an immersive digital experience that draws readers into the world of *The Uncivil War* without replacing the experience of reading it.

This is not a website.

It is not a marketing campaign.

It is an invitation.

The novel tells the story.

The experience earns the reader's curiosity.

---

# We Believe

We believe curiosity is more powerful than explanation.

We believe silence is a design element.

We believe restraint creates trust.

We believe authenticity is more compelling than spectacle.

We believe physical objects tell stories without speaking.

We believe history feels heavier when it is handled instead of displayed.

We believe visitors should leave with more questions than they arrived with.

---

# The Novel Comes First

The website never answers a question that the novel answers better.

Every artifact should suggest.

Never explain.

Every interaction should deepen curiosity.

Never reduce mystery.

If there is a choice between making the website more interesting or protecting the novel—

the novel wins.

Every time.

---

# The Visitor

The visitor is not a customer.

The visitor is not a gamer.

The visitor is not an audience member.

The visitor is the first civilian granted access to newly declassified records following the end of a seven-year American civil war.

Everything they experience should reinforce that role.

---

# The Archive

The archive is not abandoned.

It is preserved.

The war is over.

The country survived.

Enough time has passed for history to begin replacing memory.

The archive exists because someone decided the public deserved to know.

---

# Cabinet Zero

Cabinet Zero is not a museum exhibit.

It is a conservation workspace.

Objects are being preserved.

Catalogued.

Studied.

Protected.

The visitor has been allowed inside that process.

---

# Artifacts

Artifacts are evidence.

Not exposition.

Every artifact must satisfy three rules.

1. Raise more questions than it answers.

2. Reveal no major plot points.

3. Reward close inspection.

If an artifact explains too much—

it belongs in the novel instead.

---

# Motion

Motion exists only to support narrative.

Nothing moves because movement is fashionable.

Objects possess weight.

Paper slides.

Folders open.

Light settles.

Everything behaves as though it exists in the physical world.

---

# Typography

Typography is voice.

Government documents speak differently than memories.

Quotes speak differently than reports.

Official language should feel institutional.

Personal language should feel human.

The transition between those voices mirrors the journey of the novel.

---

# Atmosphere

Atmosphere is content.

Rain.

Silence.

Paper.

Brass.

Leather.

Concrete.

Filtered daylight.

These are not decorations.

They are storytelling tools.

---

# Simplicity

Every page has one visual hero.

Every interaction has one purpose.

Every artifact has one story to imply.

Complexity belongs inside the novel.

Clarity belongs inside the archive.

---

# Success

Success is not measured by clicks.

Success is measured by curiosity.

The ideal visitor leaves thinking:

"I need to know what happened."

The only place that question can be answered is inside *The Uncivil War*.

---

# Our Standard

If HBO commissioned an official companion experience for *The Uncivil War*,

this project should feel worthy of standing beside it.

If a feature does not reach that standard,

it does not ship.

---

# Final Principle

The website creates questions.

The novel creates understanding.

Never confuse the two.
